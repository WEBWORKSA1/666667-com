Quick answer: In Chinese, 6 (六, liù) sounds like 溜 / 流 — “smooth, flowing” — and online “666” means “awesome, well played”. 7 (七, qī) echoes 起 (qǐ, “rise”). So 666667 reads like a wish: everything goes smoothly… and then rises. It contains no 4, so it carries no “death” connotation.

The slang side: 666 is praise

In Chinese gaming, livestreams and short-video comments, typing 666 (or a long string like 6666666) is the standard way to cheer a great play or a clever move. It comes from 溜 (liū), which describes something done slickly and skilfully. For Western readers, “666” may bring to mind the “number of the beast”, but in Chinese online culture it is a compliment.

The traditional side: 六六大顺

The idiom 六六大顺 (liù liù dà shùn) wishes someone smooth progress in everything. That's why 6 is popular in business phone numbers, opening dates and wedding dates, and why a run of sixes feels auspicious.

Why the 7 matters

Seven (七, qī) is close in sound to 起 (qǐ), “to rise / get up / start”, and to 齐 (qí), “together, complete”. After five sixes, the 7 reads as the moment things take off. One nuance: the seventh lunar month is Ghost Month in traditional belief, so some families avoid 7 for weddings in that month — but as the final digit of a number it is broadly positive.
