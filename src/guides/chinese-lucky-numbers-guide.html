Why numbers carry meaning

Mandarin has a relatively small set of syllables and many homophones, so a number is easily “heard” as another word. Over centuries, those sound-alikes turned digits into symbols — and today they shape prices, phone numbers, floors, dates and gifts across Chinese-speaking communities worldwide.

Combinations that matter

  • 168 — 一路发, prosper all the way (business favourite)
  • 518 — 我要发, I will prosper
  • 1688 — prosper all the way, doubly
  • 520 — I love you; 1314 — for a lifetime
  • 888, 8888 — maximum prosperity
  • 14 — “will die”; 748 — “go die”; 250 — “fool”

Real-life uses

  • Prices: ending in 8 or 88 is standard; avoid 4 and 250. Try the lucky price generator.
  • Phone numbers & plates: repeating 8s and 6s and no 4 command premiums in auctions.
  • Buildings: many towers skip floors 4, 14, 24 (and sometimes 13 for Western tenants).
  • Dates: 8/8, 5/20 and 6/18 are popular for launches and weddings; the 2008 Beijing Olympics opened at 8:08 pm on 8/8/08.
  • Gifts & red envelopes: even amounts, 6/8/9-based numbers, never 4. See our red envelope guide.

Regional differences

Cantonese speakers (Hong Kong, Guangdong, much of the older diaspora) sometimes read numbers differently — 3 sounds closer to 生 (life) and 8 to 發 (prosper) in both. Younger internet users add slang like 666, 233 and 886. When in doubt, ask for a review.
