Quick answer: Numbers need no English or pinyin, work across dialects, are fast to type and carry lucky meanings. Buyers pay most for short numbers, .com, no 4, repeating 8/6/9 and meaningful combos like 168 or 518.

The input problem

Typing Chinese requires an input method: you type pinyin or strokes and pick the right characters. Domain names, however, are Latin-script. For many users, remembering an English word or a pinyin spelling is harder than remembering a number. That's why big Chinese web brands such as 163.com (NetEase's email) and 360.cn built household names on digits.

Scarcity

There are only 100 two-digit .coms and 1,000 three-digit .coms. As short numbers were absorbed, investors moved into four-, five- and six-digit names, often filtering by “Chinese premium” digits (no 0 and no 4).

Value drivers, ranked

  1. Length (2N ≫ 3N ≫ 4N ≫ 5N ≫ 6N)
  2. Extension (.com first; .cn and .net follow)
  3. No digit 4 (and for some buyers, no 0)
  4. Patterns (AAAA, AABB, ABAB, repeating runs)
  5. Meaning (168, 518, 520, 888, 1314, 666)
  6. Leading digit (6, 8, 9 preferred; leading 0 discounted)
