Quick answer: End prices in 8 (or 88/888), use 6 and 9 as alternatives, never include 4, avoid 250, and anchor bundles on meaningful numbers like 168, 518 and 1688.

Why it matters

Charm pricing isn't new — Western retail uses .99. In Chinese-speaking markets, the “charm digit” is 8. A culturally fluent price quietly signals that your brand understands its customer; a 4 in the wrong place can quietly undermine trust. This matters for sellers in mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan and Singapore — and for businesses serving large Chinese communities in North America, the UK and Australia.

The five rules

  1. End in 8. ¥98, $168, $1,288. 6 and 9 are strong alternatives.
  2. Remove every 4. $44.99 → $48.88 or $39.99. Check SKUs, model numbers and promo codes too.
  3. Mind the whole number, not just the ending: $1,458 still carries a 4.
  4. Use phrase numbers for hero offers: 168 (prosper all the way), 518 (I will prosper), 520 (love) for romance products.
  5. Avoid insults: 250 (fool) and 748 (go die) read badly even as prices.
